EXPLANATION:

Reference checking is not part of the standard. Please use the flag "/nrf" to
turn off reference checking if these kinds of references are to be passed
without error.

This is a side effect of the IP policy not to have warning messages, but to
either skip such checks, or produce an error. In other environments, I have
seen programmers get used to seeing warnings in compilations, resulting in
serious errors mixed with irrelivant errors.

There are a couple of situations where you really need to leave unreferenced
symbols in the program. In your example, if you wanted to "placehold" the
other (unreferenced) months, you do this:

program iptest06(output);
type month=(jan,feb,mar,apr,may,jun,jul,aug,sep,oct,nov,dec);
var t:array [month] of 28..31;
    m:month;
    i:1..31;
begin
  refer(feb, mar, apr, may, jun, jul, aug, sep, oct, nov);
  for m:=jan to jul do
    t[m]:=31-ord(m) mod 2;
  for m:=aug to dec do
    t[m]:=30+ord(m) mod 2;
  t[feb]:=28;
  for m:=jan to dec do begin
    for i:=1 to t[m] do
      write('*');
    writeln
  end
end.

Of course, this is non-standard. The "refer" procedure just causes every
symbol enclosed to be referenced, so it solves this problem, and problems
like where you want to have a parameter in a procedure that you are not
going to use until later, or a dummy procedure/function.

It is not documented in the demo docs.

The other topic, and I suspect what you meant, is "why can't the compiler
just leave certain usages, like unused enumerations alone" (perhaps with
a rule such as if any other members are referenced) ? I see that
point, but I am uncomfortable with the idea. It turns compiler reference
checking from black and white to shades of grey, and complicates the
understanding of the reference check facillity for users. Instead of
"it is or is not referenced" we have to talk about the context of the
reference.

COMMENT:

Try this one:

program test(output);

var f: file of record end;
    i: integer;

begin

   assign(f, 'file_of_empty_records');
   rewrite(f);
   for i := 1 to 10 do put(f);
   reset(f);
   i := 0;
   while not eof(f) do begin

      get(f);
      i := i+1

   end;
   writeln('Count of records in file: ', i:1)

end.

Why does that work ? Because records exist in the file even if they are empty.

By the way, the ISO 7185 standard way to state your test above is:

program iptest19(f);
var f:file of record end;
begin
  rewrite(f);
  put(f)
end.

The reason is that IP Pascal knows how to link header files to actual files.
You execute the above this way:

iptest19 file_of_empty_records
